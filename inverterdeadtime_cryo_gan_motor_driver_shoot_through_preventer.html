1 Cryogenic 2DEG Mobility & Turn-On Propagation Shortening

At deep cryogenic temperatures down to $-140^\circ\text{C}$ (133 K), optical phonon scattering inside the AlGaN/GaN heterostructure freezes out completely. Consequently, the low-field electron mobility $\mu_n(T)$ in the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) increases drastically by over $300\%$, dropping channel on-state resistance $R_{DS(\text{on})}$ and causing gate threshold charges to dissipate in a fraction of nominal room-temperature switching times.

$$\mu_n(T) = \mu_{0} \left( \frac{T}{T_0} \right)^{-\alpha} \implies t_{\text{prop,on}}(T) = t_{\text{prop,0}} \cdot \left[1 - \kappa \ln\left(\frac{T_0}{T}\right)\right]$$

Where $T_0 = 298.15\text{ K}$, $\alpha \approx 1.8 - 2.2$ for pure crystalline GaN, and $t_{\text{prop,on}}$ shrinks from $12.4\text{ ns}$ down to $3.4\text{ ns}$. If the dead-time programmed in the motor controller assumes room-temperature slew rates, the complementary switches overlap during transient commutation.

2 Inverter Leg Shoot-Through Cross-Conduction Energy

When high-side switch $Q_H$ turns on before low-side switch $Q_L$ has fully swept out its depletion charges, a catastrophic low-impedance short-circuit path forms directly across the high-voltage DC bus ($V_{DC} = 100\text{--}400\text{ V}$). The instantaneous shoot-through current surge is limited only by stray parasitic loop inductance $L_{\sigma}$ and dynamic channel resistance.

$$E_{\text{shoot}} = \int_0^{t_{\text{overlap}}} V_{DC} \cdot \left[ \frac{V_{DC}}{R_{DS,H} + R_{DS,L}} \left(1 - e^{-\frac{(R_H+R_L)t}{L_\sigma}}\right) \right] dt$$

Even sub-nanosecond overlap produces instantaneous localized current pulses exceeding $120\text{ A}$, inducing severe $L\frac{di}{dt}$ overvoltage transients that rupture gate insulation and destroy the lunar robotic actuator.

3 The Diagnostic $(D, \theta)$ Dynamic State Inversion

Direct physical sensing of sub-nanosecond current spikes inside hermetic cryo-actuators requires invasive Rogowski coils that fail under vacuum. Instead, our software monitors the instantaneous correlation between the PWM duty cycle $D(t)$ and the Permanent Magnet Synchronous Motor (PMSM) electrical phase angle $\theta_e(t)$ via back-EMF flux linkage.

$$\theta_e(t) = \tan^{-1}\left( \frac{-\hat{\lambda}_\beta(t)}{\hat{\lambda}_\alpha(t)} \right), \quad \sigma_\theta(D) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^N \left(\theta_{e,k} - \bar{\theta}_{e}(D_k)\right)^2}$$

In a pristine switching regime, the trajectory $(D, \theta)$ traces a smooth, bounded elliptical manifold. Microscopic switch timing overlap introduces high-frequency commutation phase jitter $\sigma_\theta$. Exceeding $1.80^\circ$ triggers instantaneous dead-time expansion.

4 Autonomous Adaptive Dead-Time Recalculation Engine

Rather than setting conservative fixed dead-times (which degrade inverter output voltage linearity, create motor torque ripple, and waste harmonic energy), the firmware executes a real-time iterative optimization law clocked synchronously with the PWM carrier period:

$$t_{dt}(k+1) = t_{dt}(k) + \gamma_1 \cdot \text{sign}\left(\sigma_\theta - \sigma_{\text{threshold}}\right)\Delta t_{\text{res}} + \gamma_2 \cdot \left(\frac{T_{\text{ref}} - T_{\text{cryo}}}{T_{\text{ref}}}\right)\delta$$

Where $\Delta t_{\text{res}} = 1.25\text{ ns}$ (high-resolution timer granularity). The observer guarantees optimal dead-time clearance over the entire $-140^\circ\text{C}$ to $+85^\circ\text{C}$ lunar diurnal cycle while maintaining $99.1\%$ inverter power efficiency.
